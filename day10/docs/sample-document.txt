Title: The Aurora Coffee Company — Field Handbook (2026 Edition)

Company Overview
Aurora Coffee Company was founded in 2014 in Galle, Sri Lanka, by Nimali Perera and
Dassanayake Fernando. The company began as a single roastery serving three cafes and
has since grown to 42 locations across Sri Lanka, India, and Singapore. Aurora's stated
mission is "to make single-origin coffee accessible without making farmers invisible."
The head office moved from Galle to Colombo in 2019. As of January 2026, Aurora employs
1,180 people, of whom 64 percent are women. The current CEO is Nimali Perera; Dassanayake
Fernando stepped back to the role of Chief Sourcing Officer in 2021.

Sourcing and the Farmer Promise
Aurora buys green coffee directly from 27 partner farms. The flagship relationship is with
the Hapugala cooperative in the Sri Lankan central highlands, which has supplied Aurora since
2015. Under the "Farmer Promise," Aurora pays a minimum of 1.8 times the local market rate
and commits to three-year purchase contracts so growers can plan ahead. Ten percent of every
wholesale invoice is redirected into a Community Fund that has, to date, financed two schools,
a water-filtration plant, and 14 micro-loans for women-led farms. Coffee is harvested between
November and February; the cherries are wet-processed within 12 hours of picking to protect
flavour clarity.

Roasting and Products
The Colombo roastery runs two German-built Probat roasters and processes about 9 tonnes of
green coffee per week. Aurora sells four core blends: Dawn (light roast, floral), Highland
(medium, chocolate and citrus), Midnight (dark, smoky), and Decaf Ember (Swiss-water
processed). The best-selling product by volume is Highland, which accounts for 38 percent of
retail sales. In 2025 Aurora launched a canned cold-brew line that reached 1.2 million units
sold in its first year. All retail bags are 250 grams and use compostable kraft packaging
introduced in 2023, which cut the company's plastic use by an estimated 71 percent.

Sustainability Targets
Aurora has committed to being carbon-neutral across its own operations (Scope 1 and 2) by
2028. Roughly 55 percent of café energy currently comes from rooftop solar. The company runs
a cup-return scheme: customers who bring a reusable cup receive a 40-rupee discount, and the
scheme diverts an estimated 600,000 single-use cups from landfill each year. Spent coffee
grounds are collected from all Colombo cafes and sold to a mushroom farm in Kadawatha, which
uses them as a growing substrate.

People and Culture
Every new barista completes a two-week training program called "First Pour," which ends with
a blind cupping exam; trainees must correctly identify at least four of six origins to pass.
Aurora offers all full-time staff a profit-share bonus paid each April, and store managers
receive an additional quarterly bonus tied to customer-satisfaction scores rather than sales
volume. The company observes a four-day work week for head-office staff, a policy piloted in
2024 and made permanent in 2025 after productivity held steady and reported burnout fell.

Challenges and Outlook
Aurora's biggest operational risk is climate volatility in the highlands: a 2023 drought cut
the Hapugala harvest by nearly a third, forcing Aurora to draw on reserve contracts in
Karnataka, India. Currency fluctuation also complicates pricing for imported equipment and
packaging. Looking ahead, Aurora plans to open its first European location in Lisbon in late
2026 and to publish its first independently audited sustainability report the same year.
Leadership has said the company will not franchise: every café remains directly owned to keep
quality and the Farmer Promise enforceable.
